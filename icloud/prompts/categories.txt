KEEP: they may still need to read it, reply, act on it, or look at it again soon. This covers mail a real person wrote to them personally, and anything with a deadline, appointment, event, payment due or delivery that is still in the future. It also covers anything that asks them a question that is still open.

ARCHIVE: it mattered once but is now in the past, or it is a record worth keeping that needs no attention, such as receipts, order and shipping confirmations, statements and tickets for past trips.

DELETE: it never had lasting value and they would not want even a summary. Examples: automated security or dependency alert digests, fundraising and campaign appeals.

UNSUBSCRIBE: a mailing list or marketing sender they do not want to hear from at all. Examples: retail and brand promotions they never engage with, political party mail, event-ticket marketing, social site digests.

SUMMARIZE: a sender they want to stay informed about without reading the whole email. Pick the level:
- low: marketing or newsletters of low interest, such as airline promotions, athletics department partner mail, deal clubs, developer newsletters, social network notifications.
- moderate: of some interest, such as a club or community newsletter, programs for kids, a local shop's sales.
- high: of real interest, such as announcements from their department or offers for tools they use.
- confirmations: notices they expect, such as meeting recordings and assets being ready.
